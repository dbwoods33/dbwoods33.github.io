A habit tracker is a grid of habits and days with a tick when you do the thing. The value is in seeing the chain, and in a score that tells you honestly how the month went. This template keeps it to one clean page so it’s quick to open and quicker to tick.

What’s in the free habit tracker template

The workbook has a main tab where you do everything and a How to use tab with one-page instructions. Here’s what you get:

  • A month picker: type the first day of the month and the day numbers and weekday letters adjust to 28, 29, 30 or 31 days. Days that don’t exist are greyed out.
  • Up to ten habits, one per row, with yellow name cells to type over.
  • Checkbox-style cells: each day has a ✓ drop-down, and any letter you type counts too. Ticked cells turn green; weekends get a faint tint.
  • Days done and monthly % for every habit: 80% or more turns green, under 50% turns amber.
  • An overall score for all your habits together.

The six habits and their ticks are sample data, labelled at the top of the sheet, so you can see the colours and percentages working. To start fresh, type over the names, then select the grid and press Delete.

How to open it in Google Sheets

The template is a standard Excel file (.xlsx). Google Sheets converts it in a few seconds, and the formulas, colours and drop-downs carry over.

  1. Download the template to your computer.
  2. Go to drive.google.com, click New → File upload and choose free-habit-tracker.xlsx.
  3. Right-click the uploaded file and choose Open with → Google Sheets.
  4. Click File → Save as Google Sheets. This makes a native copy you can edit on your phone and share.

Already have a spreadsheet open? File → Import → Upload works too: pick the file, then choose Insert new sheet(s).

How to use it in Excel

  1. Download the file and double-click to open it in Excel (Windows or Mac, 2016 or newer, or Microsoft 365).
  2. If a yellow Protected View bar appears, click Enable Editing. Excel shows this for every downloaded file; there are no macros in this one.
  3. Use File → Save As to save your own copy.

Prefer Excel online? Upload the file to OneDrive and open it in the browser. It also opens in LibreOffice Calc, which we used to test every formula.

How to use it, in four steps

1. Pick fewer habits than you want to

Three to five is plenty. Each one should be specific enough to tick yes or no: “walk 20 minutes”, not “exercise more”. Room for ten is there for flexibility, not as a target.

2. Decide your minimum version

Write habits so a bad day still counts: “read 10 pages” rather than “read a chapter”. A habit you can do on your worst day is one you’ll keep.

3. Tick at the same time every day

Pin the sheet on your phone (the Google Sheets app works well) and tick before bed. Missing a day is normal; missing two in a row is the thing to avoid.

4. Review at month end

Look at the monthly %. Keep habits above 80%, simplify anything under 50%, and only add a new one when the others feel automatic. Then duplicate the tab for next month.

Frequently asked questions

Is the habit tracker template really free?

Yes. It’s a direct .xlsx download with no email, account or signup.

Does it have real checkboxes in Google Sheets?

Not Google’s checkbox control: .xlsx files can’t carry those, so each day uses a ✓ drop-down instead (or type any letter). It works the same in Sheets and Excel. Don’t convert the grid with Insert → Checkbox, because unticked boxes hold FALSE, which the Done count would treat as done.

How do I use it in Google Sheets?

Download the .xlsx file, upload it to Google Drive, then right-click it and choose Open with → Google Sheets. Choose File → Save as Google Sheets for an editable copy you can tick from your phone.

Does it work in Excel?

Yes, in Excel for Windows and Mac, Excel for the web and LibreOffice Calc. It uses standard formulas (SUMPRODUCT, EOMONTH, WEEKDAY, CHOOSE) and no macros.

How is the monthly % calculated?

Days ticked ÷ days in the month. Ticks typed into greyed-out days that don’t exist in that month (for example the 31st in November) are ignored.

How do I track the next month?

Duplicate the Habits tab (right-click the tab → Duplicate in Google Sheets, or Move or Copy in Excel), change the MONTH cell and clear the ticks.
