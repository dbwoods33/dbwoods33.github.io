The debt snowball is simple to describe and tedious to work out by hand: pay the minimum on everything, put every spare dollar on the smallest balance, and when it’s gone, roll its payment into the next one. Doing that roll-over month by month on paper is where most people give up. This template does it for you, and shows the result on one page.

What’s in the free debt snowball template

The workbook has a main tab where you do everything and a How to use tab with one-page instructions. Here’s what you get:

  • Your debts: up to eight, each with a balance, APR and minimum payment. List them in any order.
  • One extra-payment box: the amount you can add on top of your minimums. Minimums plus extra stays fixed every month, so freed-up minimums roll into the next debt automatically.
  • Snowball order: smallest balance first, with your current target highlighted in green.
  • A paid-off month for every debt, plus the interest each one will cost you.
  • Results: months to debt-free, your debt-free date, total interest and total paid.
  • A warning if any minimum payment doesn’t cover that debt’s monthly interest.
  • Payoff schedule tab: the balance of every debt at the end of every month, so you can watch the snowball roll.

The four debts in the yellow cells are sample data, labelled at the top of the sheet: $19,800 across a store card, a personal loan, a credit card and a car loan, with $300 a month extra. They’re the same example we use in our snowball vs avalanche guide, so you can check the spreadsheet against the worked numbers there: debt-free in 26 months with $2,865.91 of interest.

How to open it in Google Sheets

The template is a standard Excel file (.xlsx). Google Sheets converts it in a few seconds, and the formulas, colours and drop-downs carry over.

  1. Download the template to your computer.
  2. Go to drive.google.com, click New → File upload and choose free-debt-snowball-calculator.xlsx.
  3. Right-click the uploaded file and choose Open with → Google Sheets.
  4. Click File → Save as Google Sheets. This makes a native copy you can edit on your phone and share.

Already have a spreadsheet open? File → Import → Upload works too: pick the file, then choose Insert new sheet(s).

How to use it in Excel

  1. Download the file and double-click to open it in Excel (Windows or Mac, 2016 or newer, or Microsoft 365).
  2. If a yellow Protected View bar appears, click Enable Editing. Excel shows this for every downloaded file; there are no macros in this one.

Prefer Excel online? Upload the file to OneDrive and open it in the browser. It also opens in LibreOffice Calc, which we used to test every formula.

How to use it, in four steps

1. List every debt, with today’s balance

Use the balance from your latest statement or app, the APR (not the monthly rate) and the minimum payment. Include everything except your mortgage: cards, store cards, personal loans, car loans, student loans, buy-now-pay-later plans.

2. Choose an extra amount you can keep up

The extra is what makes the snowball work, but it has to be sustainable. A steady $100 every month beats $400 for two months. If you’re not sure, start low; you can raise it any time and the dates update.

3. Pay in the order shown

Each month, pay the minimum on every debt and send the minimum plus all the extra to #1. When #1 is paid off, its whole payment moves to #2. Never drop the total: that roll-over is the snowball.

4. Update balances once a month

After your payments go through, type the new balances over the old ones. Real interest and fees rarely match a projection exactly, so a monthly refresh keeps the dates honest. If you get a windfall, put it on #1 and watch the debt-free date move.

Frequently asked questions

Is the debt snowball template really free?

Yes. It’s a direct .xlsx download with no email, account or signup. Use it for your own debts as often as you like.

How do I use the debt snowball template in Google Sheets?

Download the .xlsx file, upload it to Google Drive, then right-click it and choose Open with → Google Sheets. Choose File → Save as Google Sheets to get a fully editable copy.

Does it work in Excel?

Yes. It uses standard formulas only (SUM, SUMPRODUCT, COUNTIF, INDEX, MATCH, EDATE and ROUND) and no macros, so it works in Excel for Windows and Mac, Excel for the web and LibreOffice Calc.

How accurate are the payoff dates?

They’re a projection. The sheet adds interest each month at APR ÷ 12, rounded to the cent, and assumes your payments, rates and balances stay the same. Real statements differ slightly because of daily interest, fees and new charges, so update your balances monthly and the dates will track reality.

What if two debts have the same balance?

The one higher in your list goes first. If you’d rather pay the higher-interest one first in a tie, list it above the other.

Can it do the avalanche method?

No, this free version calculates the snowball only. The premium Debt Payoff Tracker runs snowball and avalanche side by side and shows the interest difference.
