Most free budget templates are either a bare list of bills or a 12-tab monster you abandon in week two. This one sits in between: one page, the four sections every household budget needs, and formulas that tell you straight away whether you’re on track.

What’s in the free budget template

The workbook has two tabs: Budget, where you do everything, and How to use, a one-page set of instructions. The Budget tab has:

  • Income: expected vs received for up to four sources (paychecks, side income, anything else), with a “Short” flag if a payment comes in low.
  • Fixed bills: rent or mortgage, utilities, phone, insurance, car payment, subscriptions and debt payments.
  • Variable spending: groceries, transport, dining out, household, personal care, entertainment, shopping, gifts and miscellaneous.
  • Savings: emergency fund, retirement and sinking funds, treated as a planned expense rather than whatever is left over.
  • Budget vs actual on every line: a Left column (budget minus actual) that turns red when you overspend, and an On track / Over status.
  • Summary: planned vs actual totals, money left over and your savings rate, plus a check that tells you whether every dollar of income has been assigned.
  • One chart: planned vs actual for income, bills, spending and savings.

Every section has a spare row, and every category name can be changed. The numbers in the yellow cells are sample data, clearly labelled at the top of the sheet, so you can see the formulas working before you type over them.

How to open it in Google Sheets

The template is a standard Excel file (.xlsx), which Google Sheets converts in a few seconds. Formatting, formulas and the chart all carry over.

  1. Download the template to your computer.
  2. Go to drive.google.com, click New → File upload and choose free-monthly-budget-template.xlsx.
  3. Right-click the uploaded file and choose Open with → Google Sheets.
  4. Click File → Save as Google Sheets. This makes a native Google Sheets copy you can edit on your phone and share with a partner.

Already have a spreadsheet open? File → Import → Upload works too: pick the file, then choose Insert new sheet(s) to add the budget to an existing workbook.

How to use it in Excel

  1. Download the file and double-click to open it in Excel (Windows or Mac, 2016 or newer, or Microsoft 365).
  2. If a yellow Protected View bar appears, click Enable Editing. Excel shows this for every file downloaded from the internet; there are no macros in this one.
  3. Use File → Save As to save your own copy, for example Budget 2026.xlsx.

Prefer Excel online? Upload the file to OneDrive and open it in the browser. It also opens in LibreOffice Calc, which we used to test every formula.

How to budget with it, in four steps

The spreadsheet does the arithmetic. These four habits are what make a budget actually work.

1. Start from take-home pay

Put what actually lands in your account in the Expected column, after tax and deductions. If your income varies, use a typical low month; anything extra becomes a bonus rather than a shortfall.

2. Give every dollar a job

Fill in a budget for each bill, spending category and savings goal until the summary says “Planned left over is $0: every dollar has a job.” This is zero-based budgeting, and it works because nothing drifts into “whatever’s left”. Savings go in as a line item, so they happen first instead of last.

3. Don’t forget irregular costs

Car registration, annual subscriptions, birthdays and holidays wreck budgets because they don’t arrive monthly. Add up what they cost in a year, divide by 12 and put that in Sinking funds.

4. Update it once a week

Open your banking app, add up each category and type the running totals into Actual. Ten minutes a week is enough. When a Left cell turns red, decide: move money from a category with slack, or ease off for the rest of the month. At month end, duplicate the tab, clear Actual and adjust the plan using what you learned.

Frequently asked questions

Is the budget template really free?

Yes. It is a direct download with no email, account or signup. Use it for your own household budget as often as you like.

How do I use this budget template in Google Sheets?

Download the .xlsx file, upload it to Google Drive, then right-click it and choose Open with → Google Sheets. Choose File → Save as Google Sheets to get a fully editable copy. You can also open a blank sheet and use File → Import → Upload.

Does it work in Excel for Mac or Excel online?

Yes. It uses only standard formulas (SUM, IF, N, AND, ROUND and TEXT) and no macros, so it works in Excel for Windows and Mac, Excel for the web and LibreOffice Calc.

How do I track more than one month?

Duplicate the Budget tab at the start of each month, rename it (for example Dec 2026) and clear the Actual column. The premium Monthly Budget Planner keeps every month in one file with a month and year selector instead.

Can I change the categories?

Yes. Type over any category name, clear the ones you don’t need, or use the empty yellow row at the bottom of each section. Totals, the summary and the chart include every row automatically.

Is my budget data private?

Yes. The file lives on your computer or in your own Google Drive. Nothing you type is sent to us.
